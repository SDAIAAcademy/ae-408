Two ways to build a voice agent

A cascade passes text between three models; speech-to-speech is one model

Cascaded

Detector, recogniser, language model, voice. Every stage can be measured, swapped and checked, and there is a transcript of what the caller said.

Speech-to-speech

Audio in, audio out, with its own turn detection. Fewer hand-offs and usually a faster first audio; the stages are hidden inside one model.

Rahhal today is cascaded on hosted open-weight models. Lab 3.2 compares it with traces of a hosted speech-to-speech session.

Keep this vendor-neutral: hosted speech-to-speech sessions are offered by several providers and change often, and the course has no key for one, so the speech-to-speech traces are synthetic and described as such. Frameworks such as Pipecat and LiveKit Agents assemble either design; the labs use plain Python so every stage is visible.

The other side of the trade

Speech-to-speech gives up the text you evaluated yesterday

  • No separate transcript to slice by dialect and channel, as in Lab 2.3.
  • No text to check before a tool call: the booking arguments come out of one model.
  • One vendor's voice, turn detection and model, changed together.
  • Usually priced per minute of audio, which Lab 4.3 compares.

Some speech-to-speech services do return a transcript; if so, check how it is produced before slicing on it. Ask Ghusoun Al-Mutlaq's question from the case study: where is the caller's audio processed and kept? The decision record must answer it for either design.

Checkpoint

What do you fix first?

A cascade has a median time to first audio of 1,550 ms. In seven of twenty turns the voice starts only after the model has written the whole reply. What change do you make first?

Swap the recogniser for a faster one
Move the servers closer to the callers
Switch to a smaller language model
Start the voice on the first sentence of the reply
Why. The trace shows the voice waiting for a full reply in seven turns. Streaming the first sentence removes that wait without changing any model: in Lab 3.2 the median fell to 1,228 ms.

Answer: D. A, B and C each move a stage that the trace shows is not where the time goes. The habit to build is: read the per-stage table before changing anything.

Common problems: summing durations of overlapping stages (the model and the voice overlap when streaming); using the end of the model's full reply as time to first audio; the 90th percentile of twenty values, which pandas interpolates. Early finishers do the stretch: a budget per stage. The first-token share breaks most often, because of the tool turns.

Has the caller finished?

Every millisecond spent waiting for silence is added to the reply

  • The pipeline after the endpoint takes 620 ms on Rahhal's clock.
  • So the silence threshold can be at most 580 ms before every reply is late.
  • Callers pause up to 1,096 ms inside a turn, longest before a number or a date.

Use what was said, not only the silence

Wait longer only when the words say the caller is mid-phrase

HOLD_WORDS = {"for", "on", "to", "and",
              "لعدد", "يوم", "على", "في", "و"}

def hold(text_before):
    last = text_before.split()[-1]
    return 600 if last in HOLD_WORDS else 0

The rule on the slide is shortened; the lab also holds after a digit word that may continue ("four", "أربعة") and after a letter of a code. The extra 600 ms applies only after those words, so ordinary replies keep the 500 ms threshold. Sixteen cut-ins remain: pauses after words the list does not know. A production line replaces the word list with a small end-of-turn model; Pipecat and LiveKit Agents both ship one. Arabic connectives such as و attach to the next word in writing, which is why the lab works on the recogniser's spacing.

When the caller talks over Rahhal

Stop for an interruption; keep talking through "mm"

Backchannel

"mm", "aha", إيه: 120 to 250 ms. The caller is listening. Stopping here makes the agent stutter.

Interruption

"No, the eleventh": 600 ms or more. Stop within 300 ms, and listen.

Lab 3.3 stretch. Stop after 260 ms of continuous caller speech: no false stops on the backchannels, every interruption handled within 300 ms.

The 260 ms comes from the stretch's sounds plus 40 ms to stop the audio; on real lines the right value comes from recorded calls. Echo matters in practice: the agent's own voice coming back through the phone must not count as the caller, which is why telephone stacks cancel echo before the detector. After a barge-in, the reply that was cut must be dropped from the conversation history, or the model believes the caller heard it.

Checkpoint

Why not just wait longer?

Rahhal cuts in on callers who pause before a date. The pipeline after the endpoint takes 620 ms and the budget is 1,200 ms. A colleague proposes raising the silence threshold from 500 to 900 ms for every turn. What happens?

Fewer cut-ins and no other change
Fewer cut-ins, but every reply is late and some callers give up and speak again
Faster replies, because the recogniser gets more audio
Rahhal stops reacting when the caller interrupts
Why. 900 + 620 = 1,520 ms on every turn, over the budget; in Lab 3.3 nine of 48 turns passed the caller's patience. Hold longer only where the words say the caller is mid-phrase.

Answer: B. The sweep at 900 ms gave 0 ok, 13 cut-ins, 26 late and 9 missed. Ask for the alternative: the hold rule, which raised the turns right from 24 to 32.

Common problems: counting the final silence of a turn as a pause inside it (that one is where Rahhal should answer); judging late before missed; comparing the threshold with a pause using greater-than in one place and greater-or-equal in another. Ask early finishers to add a word they would hold on in their own dialect and rerun the sweep.

The tool defends itself

The booking tool checks its arguments and answers in a sentence the agent can say

def book_visit(site_code, date, party_size, name):
    if site_code not in SITES:
        raise ToolError("that site is not one of ours")
    if d < TODAY:
        raise ToolError("that date has passed")
    if not 1 <= party_size <= 12:
        raise ToolError("a group can be 1 to 12 people")
    if booked(site_code, date) + party_size > 20:
        raise ToolError("that day is full ...")
    return store.add(...)

Checks the model cannot skip

A prompt can ask the model for valid arguments; only the tool can refuse invalid ones. The error text is written for the caller's ear.

The code is shortened: the lab also parses the date and refuses dates more than 60 days ahead. Capacity of 20 visitors a day per site is a case-study rule. Call K4 asks for a day that is already nearly full: the tool refuses, Rahhal says so, and the caller picks the next day. Tool design itself is AE-302's subject; here the point is that a voice agent's errors must be speakable.

What the filler may say

A filler says what is happening, never what has not happened yet

TrueOne moment, I am booking that. لحظة، أحجز لك الآن.
Not yet trueYour booking is confirmed. تم تأكيد حجزك.
K4 is why. The 5th was full: the tool refused, and a filler that had already said "confirmed" would have been false.

This is the third session outcome: masking a slow tool without misleading the caller. The same rule applies to "I have found a slot" before the availability check returns. The stretch adds an idempotency key so that a booking retried after a timeout is not written twice: two calls with the same key leave one booking.

Day 3 · Three things to keep

What to carry into the capstone

Speak for the ear

Spoken forms, chunked codes, under 12 seconds, and a listening test by category.

Trace before tuning

Per-stage times at the 90th percentile; stream the first sentence; hold the turn only mid-phrase.

Confirm before writing

Read back, book on a yes with the last confirmed slots, and fill silence only with what is true.
