From waveform to text

01
What a recogniser does

A recogniser turns a waveform into features, then features into text

1 · Sample16,000 values a second; a telephone line keeps 8,000
→
2 · Featuresa log-mel spectrogram: energy by pitch band, every 10 ms
→
3 · Encoderturns the frames into vectors
→
4 · Texta decoder writes tokens, or a classifier labels each frame
Telephone audio loses the upper half of the band. Sounds that differ there are the first to be confused.

Keep the signal processing brief: participants need to know that the model sees a spectrogram, that sample rate limits the bands it can see, and that 8 kHz telephone audio has nothing above 4 kHz. That is why Rahhal's telephone callers will be a slice of their own in Session 3.

Counting mistakes

Word error rate counts the cheapest set of edits from reference to transcript

referenceأبي ألغي الحجز رقم 1288 على قلعة النخيل
recogniser Aاب الغي الحجز رقم 1284 على قلعه النخيل
WER = (S + D + I) / N, from the alignment with the fewest edits

Here four substitutions over eight words: 0.5. Only one of them, the booking number, changes the booking.

Clip C001, recogniser A, as recorded in data/clips.jsonl. Walk the alignment: أبي/اب is a real error, ألغي/الغي a hamza, 1288/1284 a real and costly error, قلعة/قلعه a ta marbuta. Participants write the dynamic-programming alignment in step 3 and check it against jiwer on every clip. Ask the room which of the four errors a caller would notice: that question is Session 2.

Two ways to add up

Report the corpus rate: every word counts once

Corpus WER

All edits over all reference words. A long clip weighs more, because it has more words to get wrong.

Mean of clip WERs

Each clip weighs the same. Two errors in a four-word clip add 0.5; in a twenty-word clip, 0.1.

In Lab 2.1 the two agree to the third decimal (0.213 and 0.214 for A), because the clips have similar lengths. On real calls they do not: say which one you report.

Be honest about the lab: the synthetic clips are 6 to 12 words, so the two figures barely differ. The point is the convention, which matters on real recordings where a clip can be two words or two hundred. The capstone report states the corpus figure and the rules.

Checkpoint

How does this count?

Reference: "five people on Thursday" (4 words). Transcript: "five people Thursday please". What is the WER?

0.25: one word is missing
0.5: one deletion and one insertion over four words
0.4: two edits over five words
0.75: three words are out of place
Why. The cheapest alignment deletes "on" and inserts "please": two edits, divided by the four words of the reference.

Answer: B. C is the common slip: the denominator is always the reference. Point out that WER can exceed 1 when a recogniser inserts many words, which Session 4 shows on silence.

Why Arabic needs rules first

The same spoken word has several correct spellings

Every pair is taken from the clips. The fourth row is the exception that matters: على (on) and علي (the name Ali) sound alike in fast speech but are different words, and a rule that merges them can hide a real error. This is what the lab's stretch finds. Ask for the word each participant brought from yesterday's "next" slide.

Rules you can cite

Write each rule as a function, test it, and name the set you report

def diacritics(t):
    return re.sub("[ً-ْٰ]", "", t)

def alef(t):
    return re.sub("[أإآٱ]", "ا", t)

SETS = {
  "none":  [],
  "light": ["diacritics", "tatweel", "digits", "punct"],
  "full":  [*light, "alef", "maqsura", "ta"]}

Light

Removes what is never a different word: marks, stretching, digit forms, punctuation.

Full

Also merges letter variants. Fairer to spelling, but it can merge two real words.

The lab is AI-off: participants write these seven functions themselves, each with an example test. The supervised check of the course asks exactly this without help: compute error rates under two normalisation rules. The course's reference version is trails.normalise, used by every later lab, so a participant who did not finish still gets the same numbers tomorrow.

The catalog entry asks for error rates by dialect so that weaker service is visible before launch; this is a fairness requirement stated as an engineering deliverable. The clips per slice: Najdi 10, Hijazi 10, Gulf 8, Southern 8, MSA 8, Egyptian 6; 22 telephone, 28 wideband; 14 code-switched.

Intervals that are honest about dependence

Resample clips, not words: words of one clip share a speaker and a line

rng = np.random.default_rng(408)
idx = rng.integers(0, n, size=(reps, n))
rates = edits[idx].sum(1) / words[idx].sum(1)
lo, hi = np.percentile(rates, [2.5, 97.5])

Each draw

n clips with replacement; the slice's corpus rate on that draw.

Word resampling

Treats 474 words as independent: an interval far too narrow.

The bootstrap is a prerequisite from AE-303; what is new is choosing the unit. Errors cluster: a speaker with a strong accent or a bad line produces several errors in one clip. Overall in the lab: 0.089 with a 95% interval from 0.060 to 0.120, from 2,000 clip resamples.

Session 4

Streaming and silence

  • Detect speech frames with an energy-based voice activity detector
  • Stream segments to a recogniser and measure when text arrives
  • Catch and suppress text produced on silent segments
04
A call is not a file

A live line sends audio in frames and must decide where speech starts and ends

1 · Frames20 ms of audio at a time
→
2 · Detectis this frame speech?
→
3 · Segmentclose a segment after a short hangover
→
4 · Recognisesend the segment; text arrives

Voice activity detection

Energy above a noise floor in the labs; a small neural detector (Silero VAD) in production.

Diarisation

Who spoke when (pyannote.audio), for a call with two people on the caller's side.

The labs use an energy detector written in numpy because the house rule forbids downloading model weights, which Silero VAD and pyannote need. Both are named as production examples; both are on PyPI (silero-vad 6.2.3 and pyannote.audio 4.0.7 when checked). Diarisation is named here and not built: Rahhal's calls are one caller and one agent.

The detector of Lab 2.4

Speech is energy well above the line's own noise

db = 20 * np.log10(rms(frames) + 1e-9)
floor = np.percentile(db, 20)
active = db > floor + 18     # decibels
# keep 200 ms after the last active frame
# merge runs; drop runs under 150 ms
On the synthetic calldB
Line noise (the floor)−50
Hum−40
Threshold−32
Speech peaks−13

Levels measured on U.synthetic_call(): the 20th percentile of frame energy is about −50 dB, the hum about −40, speech peaks near −13. A margin of 18 dB puts the threshold above the hum and below speech; the detector finds exactly the six utterances. The call is synthetic: a voiced tone with a syllable envelope, no recorded or imitated voice.

The failure the detector prevents

Fed a silent stretch, a recogniser can write text nobody said

7.5 to 10.0 ssilence
transcriptاشتركوا في القناة (no-speech 0.68)
20.0 to 22.5 sa hum on the line
transcriptThank you. (no-speech 0.42)

Reported for Whisper

About 1% of transcriptions held whole invented phrases, more often for speakers with long pauses.

Why it matters here

Invented text reaches the booking agent as if the caller had said it.

The transcripts are the stand-in's, from the naive 2.5-second windows of Lab 2.4: phrases of the kind reported for Whisper on non-speech audio. The evidence card is Koenecke and colleagues, Careless Whisper (2024): roughly 1% of transcriptions contained entire hallucinated phrases, disproportionately for speakers with longer non-vocal durations, which is a fairness issue as well as an accuracy one. Sources: arXiv 2402.08021.

Numbers from the solution run of Lab 2.4 on the stand-in. The probability gate alone would have kept the hum's "Thank you." The detector alone would let through a recogniser that invents text inside a real speech segment, which the probability catches. Two independent signals is the pattern for every mitigation in this course.

The detector's one design choice

The hangover trades broken words against a later reply

HangoverSegmentsWER of the streamText arrives after speech ends
0 ms482.76172 ms
100 ms60358 ms
200 ms60462 ms
400 ms60672 ms

Without a hangover, every dip between syllables closes a segment, and every fragment invites invented text.

The stretch of Lab 2.4. A WER of 2.76 means the stream held almost three times as many wrong words as the reference had words: fragments and invented phrases. Every millisecond of hangover is added to the caller's wait, which is why tomorrow's latency budget starts here. The end of a turn is a longer version of the same decision (Lab 3.3).

Checkpoint

Where do you stop text on silence?

Your gate drops text when the recogniser's no-speech probability is above 0.6. A hum on the line still produces "Thank you." with probability 0.42. What do you add?

Lower the probability threshold to 0.4
A voice activity detector, so that only detected speech is sent at all
A larger recogniser
A filter that deletes the phrase "Thank you."
Why. The hum is not silence to the recogniser, so its own probability cannot be trusted there. A detector that never sends the hum removes the input that causes the text.

Answer: B. A would drop real speech on noisy lines. D is common in production code and hides the next invented phrase. The detector and the gate together are the mitigation the failure log records.
