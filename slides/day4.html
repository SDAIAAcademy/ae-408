A gap is a data request

Name it in the report and ask for recordings. Never fill a cell by copying items from a neighbouring one.

Lab 4.1 stretch

Keep ten items that nobody tunes on

  • Every threshold and rule of Day 3 was tuned on data you looked at; the hold-out is the check that it was not tuned to those items alone.
  • Stratify it too: a plain random 10 from the 60 held no seeded failure; one per modality on purpose gives two.
  • Look at the hold-out once, at the end, and report the result whatever it is.

The numbers come from the stretch of Lab 4.1. Ten items cannot give a precise rate; the hold-out is a check for over-fitting, not a second benchmark. If the hold-out result is far below the tuned result, report both and say which settings were tuned.

Six behaviours

cooperative, long pauses, mumbles, code-switches, interrupts, changes their mind.

Through the recogniser

Every caller turn passes through a stand-in recogniser with errors by dialect: what Rahhal hears is not what the card says.

Twenty cards: seven cooperative, three each code-switching, changing their mind and interrupting, two each with long pauses and mumbling. Cards are cheap to write, so write the callers who worry you: the one who corrects a date, the one who switches language mid-sentence. The scripted callers say exactly what the card says; the stretch lets a hosted model play the caller for variety.

What counts as success

Grade the booking in the store, not what Rahhal said

What Rahhal said

"Booked. Your reference is HTB-1437." True that a booking exists; silent about which one.

What the store holds

Exactly one booking for the caller, matching site, date and party of the final goal, after any change of mind.

Reasons, not just pass or fail: no booking, duplicate, wrong site, wrong date, wrong party.

The same idea as Day 1's reference fields: compare with ground truth, not with the system's own claims. Grading from the store also catches the opposite failure, a booking Rahhal never announced. The reasons make the failure table useful to the engineer who has to fix it.

The same twenty callers, after the fix

Twenty of twenty, and still only a lower bound of 0.84

  • The fix: book with the slots the caller last confirmed.
  • Re-run the same cards: a change that fixes one caller can break another.
  • 20/20 has a 95% interval of 0.84 to 1.00: twenty scripts cannot show the line is ready.
  • Keep the cards as a regression suite: every future change runs them.
  • The same run writes the per-stage latency trace of the twenty calls: p90 2,086 ms.

The Wilson interval for 20 of 20 starts at about 0.84. The latency trace of the twenty fixed calls (62 turns, Rahhal's modelled clock, no filler line): time to first audio p50 1,214 ms and p90 2,086 ms, 25 turns within 1,200 ms; the 90th percentile is the booking turns with the 900 ms tool. This trace is the voice agent's evidence for the capstone; Day 3's filler line and endpoint work are what bring it under budget. The point for the capstone: report the simulated result with its interval and its cards, and say what the scripts do not cover (real wording, noise, callers who give up). Frameworks such as Inspect can run such suites at scale; the labs keep it in plain Python.

Monthly: 15,660 SAR against 54,720. The break-even solves fixed / N + 0.064 = 3.04 for N. Below it the hosted design is cheaper, because a rented node costs the same whether it takes ten calls or ten thousand. The stretch varies the volume and adds a second node above 60,000 calls a month.

One page

A decision record says what was chosen, why, and what would change it

The lab's example

Cascaded for the pilot, on condition that its p90 is brought under 1,200 ms before launch: success not shown to differ, under a third of the cost, a transcript for dialect slices and argument checks, audio on a node the authority controls.

What would change it

p90 still over budget after the Day 3 fixes; volume under about 4,900 calls a month; a larger test showing a difference in success.

This is one defensible reading, not the answer. A team that weighs the 90th percentile above cost can write the opposite record from the same figures. What the assessor looks for is that the reasons trace to the table and the conditions are testable.

Re-run to the same figures

A fingerprint lets a reviewer check the figures without trusting them

def fingerprint(raw, table):
    data = raw + table.to_csv()
    return sha256(data.encode()).hexdigest()[:16]

# Fingerprint: a0c892e8bf47525f
  • The SHA-256 of the input file and the decision table.
  • A reviewer re-runs the notebook: the same fingerprint, or a difference to explain.
  • Line endings are normalised first, so Windows and Colab agree.

The course outcome asks for a record "that a reviewer re-runs to the same figures". The fingerprint makes that check one comparison. It proves the computation is repeatable, not that the inputs are right: the record still has to say where the measurements came from.

Lab 4.4, step 4

The first thing every caller hears says the voice is generated

Arabic, 4.5 sمسارات التراث. أنا رحّال، مساعد آلي بصوت مولّد.
English, 4.9 sThis is Rahhal, an automated assistant with a generated voice.
The case study's rule: first, in the caller's language, under 6 seconds, before any question about a name or a number.

The national Deepfakes Guidelines ask that synthetic content be disclosed and labelled, and that a real person's voice or likeness not be used without consent; Rahhal uses a stock generated voice, not a cloned one. The lengths are the stand-in's estimates; with a Groq key the lab measures synthesised audio. Paraphrase the guidelines in class; do not quote them as legal text.

Day 4 · Three things to keep

What to carry into the capstone

Coverage before scores

Targets first, a coverage table beside every result, and gaps named as data requests.

Grade the outcome

Simulated callers graded from the store, with transcript errors kept apart from task failures.

Decide on the record

Cost per call at a stated volume, intervals on quality, a fingerprint, and a data-handling note.
